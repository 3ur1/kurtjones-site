Facilities, workflow and technical delivery

I work where media production meets the infrastructure underneath it. I have specified, run and repaired production facilities, and I have worked on the network and data centre side that modern production depends on.

Most people in this field have one of those halves. I have both.

Running a university media facility University of West London, 2019 to 2025

Five years running the film and motion capture studios: studio floor, control room, gallery and post production suites.

  • Specified and delivered the major equipment upgrades, from requirement through procurement and installation to handover.
  • Maintained the post production estate across the editing and grading suites, and resolved faults for individuals and for whole cohorts.
  • Prepared and calibrated the studio for a range of camera formats and lighting setups, and kept it operating safely.
  • Ran all of it against a teaching timetable. A fault on the day meant an audience already booked in and nowhere for it to go.

Building a distributed live broadcast 2020 to 2021

When the campus closed, the video journalism course still had a live news programme to produce and air.

The problem. A production built around a physical gallery, with a crew that could no longer be in the same building, and a transmission slot that did not move.

What I built. A remote production workflow that let the crew run the programme with everyone in a different place: contribution, communication, direction and transmission, with the gallery functions distributed rather than abandoned.

The outcome. The programme continued to be produced and aired, on schedule, with the course running as normal.

This is the problem that most of the media technology industry is now selling into. I solved a version of it under a hard deadline with no budget for failure.

Infrastructure Network engineer, 2025 to present

Field and remote network engineering at carrier and colocation facilities across Europe and North America.

  • Install, test and troubleshoot network infrastructure on site, isolating faults step by step and recording the diagnosis and the fix.
  • Maintain documentation of system architecture and procedures so recurring issues can be resolved by others.

I am not a network specialist and do not present myself as one. The value is that I can read what is underneath a production system, and talk to the people who own it, without needing a translator.
